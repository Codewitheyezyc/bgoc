# Document 7 — Growth Strategy
### Beverly Group of Companies (BGOC)

## The core growth insight

BGOC has something most new apps don't: **seven physical stores, in one real mall, with existing customers who already trust the name.** That's not a small advantage — it means growth doesn't have to start from zero the way it would for a brand-new marketplace. The growth strategy's job is to convert existing offline trust into app usage, not to manufacture trust from scratch through ads.

This changes the whole approach: BGOC's early growth engine is **the physical stores themselves**, not paid marketing.

## Growth engine 1 — In-store conversion (the strongest lever)

Every one of the seven stores has foot traffic right now, today, at Pinnacle Mall. Each of those customers is a potential app user who doesn't need convincing that Beverly Group is trustworthy — they just need to know the app exists and be nudged to try it.

Practical tactics:
- A QR code at each store's till/counter: "Scan to order from any Beverly store — one app."
- Staff at each store mentioning the app at checkout, especially to repeat customers.
- A small first-order incentive (discount, free delivery, or bonus loyalty points) specifically for people who download in-store — this converts a walk-in customer into an app user at the exact moment trust is highest.

This engine costs almost nothing and should be running before any paid marketing starts.

## Growth engine 2 — Social media, store by store

Since Isaac already runs active social content for Cognara (carousels, infographics across LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Instagram, TikTok), the same skill set applies here — but the content strategy should differ, because BGOC's audience is local and transactional, not global and educational like Cognara's.

- **All seven stores carry the same Beverly badge/icon** — there's no separate logo per store. What varies is content style, not identity: food-related content (Beverly Exclusive Restaurant, Beverly Bakeries and Confectionery, Yurmealicious Pizza) tends to perform well as photo/video of the actual product; fashion (Celebrity Styles) performs well as short styled photo content; toys (Dollnatia, Toys in Candiland) do well around gifting occasions (birthdays, Children's Day, Christmas); Homeworld content works well as "before/after" or practical home tips.
- **Instagram and TikTok** carry the most weight here since food, fashion, and toys are highly visual — likely more important for BGOC than they were for Cognara.
- **Facebook** stays relevant for the local Asese/Pinnacle Mall community specifically, since Facebook still has strong local-community usage in many Nigerian markets.
- Cross-promotion moment: a post about Yurmealicious Pizza can end with "Order it — plus everything else Beverly Group sells — in one app," reinforcing the platform idea in every single post, not just launch content.

## Growth engine 3 — Word of mouth, engineered on purpose

Word of mouth doesn't just happen; it gets designed for.

- **Referral incentive**: "Invite a friend, you both get [X]" — simple, common pattern, works especially well once there's a wallet/credit system in place (per Document 5's architecture).
- **The combined-cart moment itself is shareable**: someone ordering pizza and a birthday gift in one checkout is a genuinely novel local experience worth talking about — "I ordered dinner and a gift for my niece from the same app" is the kind of thing people mention to friends organically, if the experience actually feels smooth.
- **Local reviews and testimonials**, especially early ones from real, recognizable Pinnacle Mall customers, carry more weight in this context than generic star ratings, because the audience is local and relationship-driven.

## Growth engine 4 — Content that teaches the platform idea itself

A real risk: people might download the app, use it for one store, and never realize the other six exist (this is the exact failure mode flagged in Document 4 and Document 6, Screen 5b). Growth content has a job here too, not just acquisition:

- Occasional posts specifically about "did you know Beverly Group also has..." — reintroducing existing app users to stores they haven't tried, not just new users to the app.
- Bundled promotions across stores ("Order dinner from Yurmealicious and get 10% off your next Homeworld purchase") that give people a concrete reason to try a second store, not just awareness of it.

## What growth strategy is not, for BGOC

- **Not a paid-ads-first strategy.** With seven physical stores and existing foot traffic, paid ads are a later amplifier, not the starting point. Spending on ads before in-store conversion and social content are working would be solving the wrong problem first.
- **Not chasing viral, broad reach.** BGOC's growth is inherently local — Asese, Pinnacle Mall, and the surrounding community. Chasing broad national virality would bring in people who can't actually receive deliveries or visit the mall, which doesn't help the business.
- **Not measured by downloads alone.** A download that never leads to a first order, or never leads to a second store, isn't real growth — Document 4's five stages (Discovery → First Open → First Order → Second Store → Habit) should shape what actually gets tracked, not just install counts.

## What to measure

- In-store QR scan-to-download conversion rate (tells you if Engine 1 is working)
- First-order completion rate after download (tells you if the app itself is trustworthy enough)
- Percentage of customers who order from a second store within their first month (the single most important number for whether the platform strategy is working, per Document 6)
- Repeat order rate at 30/60/90 days (habit formation, per Document 4's final stage)
